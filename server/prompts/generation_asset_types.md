## blurb

a back-cover blurb / product description of {size} words: a hook in the first sentence, what the book is about and why it matters, and a closing line that makes people want to read it.

## social_post

one social media post {platform}.

## social_reply

a reply to the social media post given below, {platform}. Respond to the post's actual point first, then add the book's perspective; sound like a person taking part in the conversation, not like an advertisement. Mention the book (title and author) naturally at most once. Use hashtags only if they are usual for this platform's replies.

## video_script

a script for a short vertical video {platform}, 30-60 seconds long, about the book. Structure it as: HOOK (first 3 seconds, spoken), then 3-5 numbered SCENES, each with "Visual:" (what is shown), "Voice-over:" (what is said) and "On-screen text:" (a few words), then a CTA (call to action), then a caption with fitting hashtags. Use plain labels (HOOK, SCENE 1, ..., CTA, CAPTION) without emojis; keep the on-screen text calm, without exclamation marks.

## youtube_video

the YouTube page for a video about the book, in exactly these four sections with these headings: "Title options:" with 3 numbered titles (each under 70 characters, curiosity without clickbait); "Description:" of {size} words whose first two lines work as the preview; "Chapters:" with 3-6 timestamps (e.g. "0:00 Intro") for a 6-10 minute video; "Tags:" with 10-15 comma-separated tags.

## newsletter

a newsletter section of {size} words: a subject line on the first line ("Subject: ..."), then an engaging introduction to the book and a call to action.

## ad_copy

three short ad variants. Each has a headline (max 8 words) and a body of {size} words.

## lead_magnet

a lead magnet outline: a compelling title, a one-sentence promise, and {size} bullet points of what the reader will get.

## reader_question

a post for the book's own Facebook Page that asks its readers ONE open question drawn from the book: start with 2-4 short sentences that present an idea, claim or scene from the excerpts (cited), then ask one clear question people can answer from their own view or experience, without having read the book. 40-100 words, conversational. Write as the book's author in the first person, talking to their readers (e.g. "In my book I argue ...", "While writing chapter 3 I kept asking myself ..."); never refer to the book in the third person (not "the book discusses", "the text states", "the author argues"). End with the question. No sales pitch, no "link in bio", at most one hashtag.

## persona

an audience persona for this book of {size} words: name and short description, demographics, goals, pain points, where to reach them, and why this book appeals to them.
